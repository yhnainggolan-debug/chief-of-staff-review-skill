# Presentation and room-read lens

Use for decks, live reveals, demos, web presentations, and boardroom storytelling. Communication quality is substantive when it changes comprehension, credibility, or the audience's willingness to act.

## Five-second test

For each critical frame, ask what the audience understands before the presenter explains it. The intended takeaway should be visible without requiring a paragraph of narration.

## Screen versus speaker

Separate what must be read from what should be spoken:

- Screen: headline, decisive evidence, relationship, implication, and ask.
- Speaker: nuance, transition, context, caveats, and story.

If the audience must read while listening, reduce copy. If the claim depends entirely on narration, strengthen the visual evidence.

## Sequence and pacing

Test whether each beat earns the next:

1. establish current reality
2. create tension or opportunity
3. reveal the mechanism or proposition
4. supply evidence or proof
5. translate into near-term action
6. request a decision

Flag unsupported specificity, premature ambition, repetitive beats, and momentum lost to defensive disclaimers.

## Claim-to-evidence ratio

Big language increases the burden of proof. Distinguish:

- proven capability
- current initiative or pipeline
- illustrative opportunity
- future ambition

Use wording and labels that make the category unmistakable.

## Room reaction

Simulate:

- what creates interest
- where skepticism begins
- what feels inflated or decorative
- which phrase triggers political or territorial resistance
- where the audience asks, “What changes Monday morning?”

The close should transition immediately into owners, quantified moves, milestones, and the decision required.

## Delivery and fallback

Check the actual presentation environment:

- readability at room distance
- 16:9 and target screen behavior
- keyboard, click, swipe, and presenter control
- motion that explains direction rather than decorates
- reduced-motion or static fallback
- mobile portrait and landscape if the artifact will be shared by phone
- clipping, wrapping, load failure, and browser compatibility

Do not assume shrinking a desktop composition creates a usable mobile experience. Recommend a separate responsive composition when hierarchy or flow changes materially.

## Useful anchors

Use slide, beat, scene, frame, transition, or quoted headline anchors. Include exact copy changes when wording is the problem.
